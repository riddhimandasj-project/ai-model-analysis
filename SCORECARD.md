# AI Response Evaluation Scorecard

## 1. Purpose

This scorecard provides a consistent way to evaluate AI-generated responses. It is designed for learning and exploratory experiments, not as a validated scientific measurement instrument.

## 2. Rating Scale

- **1 — Poor:** Major problems; fails the criterion.
- **2 — Weak:** Several important problems.
- **3 — Adequate:** Acceptable, but with noticeable limitations.
- **4 — Good:** Strong performance with minor issues.
- **5 — Excellent:** Meets the criterion very well, with evidence to support the rating.
- **N/A — Not assessable:** There is not enough information to give a fair rating. Explain why.

Use the same definitions for every response in a comparison.

## 3. Evaluation Criteria

### A. Accuracy
Are factual claims correct and consistent with reliable sources?

Record specific claims checked, the sources used, and any errors or uncertainty. Do not award a high score merely because an answer sounds confident.

### B. Clarity
Is the response understandable, well-organised, and easy to follow?

Consider wording, structure, explanations, and whether examples or visuals genuinely help. More detail does not automatically mean greater clarity.

### C. Relevance
Does the response answer the question and follow the requested instructions?

Record important parts of the request that were addressed or missed.

### D. Evidence
Does the response provide appropriate, verifiable support for claims when needed?

Check whether cited sources exist and actually support the claims. If no citations were requested or supplied, distinguish missing citations from claims that are independently verifiable. Do not confuse evidence quality with factual accuracy.

### E. Usefulness
Does the response help the intended reader understand the topic or complete the task?

Consider practical value, appropriate examples, actionable advice, and whether important limitations are explained.

## 5. How to Use the Scorecard

1. Define the question and evaluation criteria before comparing responses.
2. Save the exact prompts and responses.
3. Keep the AI system, model, settings, and other conditions the same where possible. If they differ, document the differences.
4. Score each criterion separately and record specific evidence for every rating.
5. Verify important factual claims using reliable, relevant sources.
6. Mark a criterion N/A if it genuinely cannot be assessed, and explain why.
7. Repeat tests with multiple questions and, where possible, multiple runs.
8. Report individual scores and meaningful differences, not just a single total.

## 6. Avoiding Misleading Comparisons

- One question is not enough to establish a general rule.
- A longer answer is not necessarily better or more accurate.
- A polished presentation does not prove factual correctness.
- Different models or settings can confound a prompt comparison.
- Repeated runs may produce different answers.
- Ratings involve judgment; explain disagreements and uncertainty.
- Do not invent scores or mark unperformed checks as complete.
- Treat small score differences cautiously. The scale is ordinal, and a difference of one point is not necessarily a measurable improvement of equal size across criteria.
- Do not treat the average score as an objective measure of overall AI quality.

## 7. Important Limitation

This is a personal learning rubric, not a validated psychometric scale or formal AI benchmark. Its value comes from consistent use, transparent evidence, and honest reporting.
